Spectral methods give rigorous bounds on Pauli contextuality, yet a spectrum need not retain the information needed by a related completion problem. For N qubits, D=2^N, and any set S of nonidentity Pauli observables, Lean 4 proves Jonah's spectral floor -- satisfied minus violated commuting triples is at most (D-2)|S|^(3/2)/(6 sqrt(D-1)), forcing asymptotic violation fraction 1/2 -- its quaternionic (Kramers) refinement, five-way saturation equivalence, and quantum-metric and two-character theorems. Outside the classical families, Lean 4 proves exact degrees 75 (4 qubits), 480 (5 qubits), 2016 (6 qubits) and 729344 (7 qubits), plus an infinite non-closed contextual floor-saturating pure-CCZ determinant family |Psi_{3m}> (m>=1), while ruling out rank-8 nine-qubit forms across 540.3 million cyclic-invariant cases. On classical geometries, Lean 4 proves exact CSS witnesses, ladders and unconditional bounds d(W(7,2))>=1455 and d(Q^+(9,2))>=6444, paired with an independent 42-orbit C census and pure-integer dual check reproducing Jonah's exact triple (315,1575,6975).

We then test Jonah's strict law: on a 71-point section of the real four-qubit quadric, two cospectral 16-by-16 carriers have 64-by-64 completions with cubic traces 14976 and 15360 and exact global binary completion minima 368 and 344 (exact gap 24, kernel-certified via pure-integer MET-SDP duals). Graph constructions over R, C and H, a cone pair swap and a universal cubic-trace identity give cospectral carriers with distinct completion eigenvalue multisets in all nine vertex cases for every N>=4, proving Jonah's Conjecture E. All main theorems (110 newly audited declarations plus retained audits) are proved in Lean 4 with standard axioms only, with exact checks through six qubits and Tsotchke demonstrations. The work is AI-assisted and is neither externally peer reviewed nor a quantum-hardware experiment.
