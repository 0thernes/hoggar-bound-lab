Spectral methods give rigorous bounds on Pauli contextuality, but a spectrum need not retain the information required by a related completion problem. For N qubits, D=2^N, and an arbitrary set S of nonidentity Pauli observables, a Lean 4 development proves Jonah's spectral floor: satisfied minus violated commuting triples is at most (D-2)|S|^(3/2)/(6 sqrt(D-1)). Saturating configurations outside the classical families have exact contextuality degrees 75 on 135 four-qubit observables, 480 on 496 five-qubit observables, and 729344 on 8128 seven-qubit observables; all three degrees are proved in Lean with kernel-checked counts. We relate saturation to pure states with a single nonzero Pauli modulus, two-character sets and flat phase states, and classify a specified third-level phase-state family through five qubits.

We then turn to Jonah's strict law: does the spectrum of a sign carrier on a hyperplane section determine the spectrum of the completion operator on the remaining observables? Two sign assignments on a 71-point section of the real four-qubit configuration have cospectral 16-by-16 carriers, but their 64-by-64 completion operators have cubic traces 14976 and 15360, and their exact binary completion minima differ. A graph construction extends this to every N>=4, with cubic traces that differ by (3/2)8^(N-1); Lean proves this case, C3, for every N>=4 in the actual Pauli model. Variants over the real, complex and quaternionic numbers and a pair swap for cones give such carriers in all nine vertex cases for every N>=4, proving Jonah's Conjecture E. Outside case C3 the proof uses two structure theorems of Jonah and is checked by exact computation up to five qubits. Independent exact verifiers, falsification controls and Tsotchke software demonstrations accompany the paper. The work is AI-assisted and is neither externally peer reviewed nor a quantum-hardware experiment.
