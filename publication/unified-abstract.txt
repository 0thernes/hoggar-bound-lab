Spectral methods give rigorous bounds on Pauli contextuality, yet a spectrum need not retain the information needed by a related completion problem. For N qubits, D=2^N, and any set S of nonidentity Pauli observables, Lean 4 proves Jonah's spectral floor -- satisfied minus violated commuting triples is at most (D-2)|S|^(3/2)/(6 sqrt(D-1)) -- together with its quaternionic (Kramers) refinement for elliptic quadrics and the five-way saturation equivalence linking equality to pure states with a single nonzero Pauli modulus. Saturating supports outside the classical families have exact contextuality degrees 75 (135 four-qubit observables), 480 (496 five-qubit observables) and 729344 (8128 seven-qubit observables), all proved in Lean with kernel-checked counts. We relate saturation to two-character sets and flat phase states, and classify a third-level phase-state family through five qubits.

We then test Jonah's strict law: does a sign carrier's spectrum on a hyperplane section determine its completion spectrum on the remaining observables? On a 71-point section of the real four-qubit quadric, two assignments give cospectral 16-by-16 carriers whose 64-by-64 completions have cubic traces 14976 and 15360 and distinct binary minima (<=344 < 347). A graph construction extends the gap to every N>=4 in case C3 (difference (3/2)8^(N-1)), and variants over R, C and H, a cone pair swap and a universal cubic-trace identity yield cospectral carriers with distinct completion spectra in all nine vertex cases for every N>=4, proving Jonah's Conjecture E. All nine cases, Corollaries 16 and 18, Lemmas 20 and 21 and the cubic-trace identity are proved in Lean 4 using standard axioms only, with exact verifiers and Tsotchke demonstrations. The work is AI-assisted and is neither externally peer reviewed nor a quantum-hardware experiment.
