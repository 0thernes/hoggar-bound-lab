Spectral methods give rigorous bounds on Pauli contextuality, yet a spectrum need not retain the information needed by a related completion problem. For N qubits, D=2^N, and any set S of nonidentity Pauli observables, Lean 4 proves Jonah's spectral floor -- satisfied minus violated commuting triples is at most (D-2)|S|^(3/2)/(6 sqrt(D-1)), forcing asymptotic violation fraction 1/2 -- its quaternionic (Kramers) refinement, five-way saturation equivalence, and quantum-metric and two-character theorems. Saturating supports outside the classical families have exact contextuality degrees 75 (135 four-qubit observables), 480 (496 five-qubit observables) and 729344 (8128 seven-qubit observables), all proved in Lean with kernel-checked counts, and we classify a third-level flat phase-state family through five qubits.

We then test Jonah's strict law: does a sign carrier's spectrum on a hyperplane section determine its completion spectrum on the remaining observables? On a 71-point section of the real four-qubit quadric, two assignments give cospectral 16-by-16 carriers whose 64-by-64 completions have cubic traces 14976 and 15360 and separated binary minima enclosed in [347,368] and [330,344] (the lower bound 330 proved via an exact QUBO SDP dual). A graph construction extends the gap to every N>=4 in case C3 (difference (3/2)8^(N-1)), and variants over R, C and H, a cone pair swap and a universal cubic-trace identity yield cospectral carriers with distinct completion eigenvalue multisets in all nine vertex cases for every N>=4, proving Jonah's Conjecture E. All nine cases, Clifford transport, Corollaries 16 and 18, Lemmas 20-21 and Theorem 22 are proved in Lean 4 with standard axioms only, plus exact checks through six qubits and Tsotchke demonstrations. The work is AI-assisted and is neither externally peer reviewed nor a quantum-hardware experiment.
