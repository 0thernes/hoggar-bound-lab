Spectral methods give rigorous bounds on Pauli contextuality, but a spectrum need not retain the information required by a related completion problem. We study both aspects together. For N qubits, D=2^N, and an arbitrary set S of nonidentity Pauli observables, a Lean 4 development proves Jonah's spectral floor: satisfied minus violated commuting triples is at most (D-2)|S|^(3/2)/(6 sqrt(D-1)). Saturating configurations outside the classical families have exact contextuality degrees 75 on 135 four-qubit observables, 480 on 496 five-qubit observables, and 729344 on 8128 seven-qubit observables. The first two degrees have kernel-checked counts; the seven-qubit counts are executable exact computations. We relate saturation to pure states with a single nonzero Pauli modulus, two-character sets and flat phase states, including an exhaustive classification of the specified third-level phase-state family through five qubits.

We then exhibit two sign assignments on a 71-point section of the real four-qubit configuration. Their 16-by-16 Pauli carriers are cospectral, but their 64-by-64 completion operators have cubic traces 14976 and 15360. This establishes the four-qubit C3 instance of Jonah's Conjecture E: the carrier spectrum alone does not determine the completion spectrum. A block certificate explains the information loss. The largest completion eigenvalues differ by two, and the associated spherical relaxations differ by 32; these are not statements about exact binary completion optima. Lean checks carrier cospectrality and completion noncospectrality in the actual Pauli model, while the printed factorizations and relaxation gaps remain algebraic results outside that formal scope. Independent exact verifiers, falsification controls and Tsotchke software demonstrations accompany the paper. The work is AI-assisted and is neither externally peer reviewed nor a quantum-hardware experiment.
