A machine-checked spectral floor for Pauli contextuality

Lean 4 formalization for every number of qubits, an independent reproduction of the October 2026 results, and an end-to-end demonstration on the Tsotchke stack

0thernes (Alexander S. Donahue), 0thernes LLC, with Claude (Anthropic) as AI research system
Hoggar Bound Lab, version 1.0, 9 November 2026. Research preprint. Not peer reviewed.

Priority note. The inequality formalized here was first posted by B. W. Jonah on 21 October 2026 (Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23538829), where it is called the spectral lower bound or spectral floor. We derived it independently on 9 November 2026 and learned of the earlier notes the same day, during our own literature audit. Priority for the bound, for its saturation by Hoggar-type states, and for the exact four- and five-qubit values belongs to the works cited in Section 1.2. What is new here is stated in Section 1.1.

Abstract

For \(N\) qubits, a noncontextual hidden-variable model assigns a value \(\pm 1\) to every Pauli observable. Quantum mechanics fixes the sign of the product of any three mutually commuting Pauli observables whose product is \(\pm I\) (a context), and no assignment can reproduce all of these signs. The contextuality degree is the least number of contexts an assignment must get wrong. We give a complete, kernel-checked Lean 4 proof (Lean 4.35, Mathlib; no axioms beyond the standard three) of the spectral floor: for every \(N \ge 1\), every set \(S\) of non-identity \(N\)-qubit Pauli observables with all of its induced contexts, and every assignment, the number of satisfied minus violated contexts is at most \((D-2)\,|S|^{3/2} / (6\sqrt{D-1})\), where \(D = 2^N\). The formal development works with the explicit \(2^N \times 2^N\) complex Pauli matrices and derives the context signs from matrix multiplication. Checked corollaries: every assignment violates at least the fraction \(1/2 - \sqrt{D+1}/(D+2)\) of all contexts of the full \(N\)-qubit Pauli configuration, for every \(N\); at least 63 of 315 contexts for three qubits (the known exact value); at least 1451 of 5355 for four qubits; at least 28786 of 86955 for five qubits; at least 315 of 1575 on the four-qubit hyperbolic quadric (the known exact value); and at least 6426 of 23715 on the five-qubit hyperbolic quadric. The last statement is, to our knowledge, the first formally verified proof that the degree of \(Q^+(9,2)\) is not \(4743\), a question raised by Li, Gupta and Ganesh and first answered, with computer assistance, by Jonah. We also reproduce the best known assignments for two to five qubits with an independent search, confirm the geometry of their violated contexts, and run the corresponding state-independent contextuality test end to end on Tsotchke's Moonlab simulator, with the quantum geometric tensor kernels of QGTL, the quantum_rng generator and the Eshkol language as independent components. Every numerical claim is a test that can fail; the repository contains the tests, a negative control and the Lean sources.

1. Scope

1.1 What is new in this work

  1. A formal proof. The spectral floor is proved in Lean 4 for every number of qubits \(N \ge 1\) and for arbitrary sets of observables with their induced contexts (theorem hoggar_bound). The proof is about the actual matrices: Pauli operators are defined as explicit complex matrices indexed by bit vectors, their product phases and commutation rule are theorems, and the bound is derived from the spectral theorem. Earlier accounts of the bound are explicitly not formally verified [J2], and the earlier Lean development of Li, Gupta and Ganesh [LGG] covers one four-qubit configuration by a different (combinatorial) argument.
  2. Formally verified numerical corollaries, listed in the abstract and in Table 2, including formal answers to two questions asked in [LGG]: the degree of \(Q^+(9,2)\) exceeds \(4743\), and the violated fraction of the four-qubit full space and of the five-qubit hyperbolic quadric is strictly above \(1/5\).
  3. A statement for arbitrary observable sets. The published statements of the floor concern line-closed families (the full space and the quadrics). The trace argument needs no closure hypothesis, and the Lean theorem has none.
  4. Consequences spelled out for all \(N\): an exponentially growing quantum-to-classical ratio with a proof (the 2010 argument for such growth rests on a lemma that fails at three qubits), a noise threshold that tends to zero, a two-sided estimate of the optimal violated fraction (Proposition 4), and a reading of violated contexts in terms of the quantum geometric tensor (Proposition 3).
  5. An independent reproduction and a running demonstration: a search engine, four independent implementations of the geometry (C, Python, JavaScript, Eshkol) and the Lean model agree on every count; the experiment runs on the Tsotchke stack with each component labelled for what it is.

1.2 What is not new, and whose it is

  • The contextuality degree and its first values: de Boutray, Holweck, Giorgetti, Masson and Saniga [dB]; Muller, Saniga, Giorgetti, de Boutray and Holweck [M1] (three-qubit degree 63, the split Cayley hexagon of violated contexts); Muller, Saniga, Giorgetti, Holweck and Kelleher [M2] (upper bounds for four to seven qubits, lower bound \(L/5\)); Saniga et al. [S] (hexagons).
  • The exact value \(315\) for the four-qubit hyperbolic quadric, its 240 optimal sets and a Lean proof of that value: Li, Gupta and Ganesh [LGG].
  • The exact values \(d(Q^-(7,2)) = 315\), \(d(W(7,2)) = 1575\), \(d(Q^+(9,2)) = 6975\) (computer-assisted), the operator \(A = \sum_v f(v) P_v\) and its Hoggar spectrum \(\{21, -3^7\}\): Jonah [J1].
  • The spectral floor itself, its Kramers (quaternionic) form for elliptic quadrics, the description of quadrics by the antiunitaries \(P_c K\), and the theorem that the floor is attained exactly for \(W(5,2)\), \(Q^+(7,2)\), \(Q^-(5,2)\) (and trivially \(Q^-(3,2)\)): Jonah [J2], with the trace formula also in [J3]. Stronger five-qubit lower bounds (\(d(W(9,2)) \ge 28830\), \(d(Q^-(9,2)) \ge 6510\)) are announced, with proofs held privately, in [J4].
  • The scalar inequality behind the floor is classical: it is Okumura's lemma [O] (equivalently the maximal sample skewness, Wilkins [W]).
  • The Hoggar lines and the cubic purity condition for quantum states: Hoggar [H], Stacey [St], Jones and Linden [JL].

Our derivation in Sections 3 to 5 was made without knowledge of [J1]–[J4]; where it coincides with them we say so. We did not re-run the exhaustive computations behind the exact values of [J1] and do not vouch for them; we only observe that every assignment we found is consistent with them.

2. Setting

Pauli operators. Let \(D = 2^N\). A point is a nonzero vector \(v = (a \mid c) \in \mathbb{F}_2^{2N}\). It labels the Hermitian operator

\[P_v \;=\; i^{\,|a \wedge c|}\, X^{a} Z^{c},\]

a tensor product of \(I, X, Y, Z\) (the exponent \(|a \wedge c|\) counts the factors equal to \(Y = iXZ\)). \(P_0 = I\), \(P_v^2 = I\), and \(\operatorname{tr} P_v = 0\) for \(v \ne 0\). Products close up to a phase,

\[P_u P_v = \tau(u,v)\, P_{u+v}, \qquad \tau(u,v) = i^{\,k_u + k_v + 3 k_{u+v}}\,(-1)^{c_u \cdot a_v}, \quad k_v = |a_v \wedge c_v| ,\]

and with the symplectic form \(\langle u, v\rangle = c_u\cdot a_v + c_v \cdot a_u\) one has \(\tau(v,u) = (-1)^{\langle u,v\rangle}\tau(u,v)\) and \(\tau(u,v)^2 = (-1)^{\langle u,v\rangle}\). So \(P_u\) and \(P_v\) commute exactly when \(\langle u,v\rangle = 0\), and then \(\tau(u,v) = s(u,v) \in \{+1,-1\}\).

Contexts. A context (a line of the symplectic polar space \(W(2N-1,2)\)) is a set \(\ell = \{u, v, u+v\}\) of three distinct points with \(\langle u, v\rangle = 0\). Its three operators commute and \(P_u P_v P_{u+v} = s_\ell\, I\) with \(s_\ell = s(u,v)\), which does not depend on the order.

Configurations and assignments. Fix a set \(S\) of points and let \(\mathcal{L}(S)\) be the set of all contexts contained in \(S\), \(L_S = |\mathcal{L}(S)|\). An assignment \(f : S \to \{+1,-1\}\) violates \(\ell\) when \(f(u) f(v) f(u+v) \neq s_\ell\). Put

\[\beta_S(f) = \sum_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}(S)} s_\ell\, f(u) f(v) f(u+v) = L_S - 2\,\mathrm{viol}(f), \qquad d(S) = \min_f \mathrm{viol}(f).\]

\(d(S)\) is the contextuality degree [dB]. For the noncontextuality inequality \(\chi = \sum_\ell s_\ell \langle P_u P_v P_{u+v}\rangle \le L_S - 2 d(S)\) quantum mechanics gives \(\chi = L_S\) in every state [C, LGG].

Three families. The full space is \(S = \mathbb{F}_2^{2N}\setminus\{0\}\), with \(D^2-1\) points and \(L = (D^2-1)(D^2-4)/12\) contexts. For \(c \in \mathbb{F}_2^{2N}\) let \(q_0(v) = |a\wedge c| \bmod 2\) and \(Q_c = \{ v \ne 0 : q_0(v) + \langle c, v\rangle = 0\}\). \(Q_c\) is a hyperbolic quadric \(Q^+(2N-1,2)\) if \(q_0(c) = 0\) and an elliptic quadric \(Q^-(2N-1,2)\) if \(q_0(c) = 1\). With \(K\) complex conjugation in the computational basis, \(\theta_c = P_c K\) is antiunitary, \(\theta_c P_v \theta_c^{-1} = (-1)^{q_0(v) + \langle c,v\rangle} P_v\) and \(\theta_c^2 = (-1)^{q_0(c)}\). So the quadrics are the sets of Pauli observables fixed by a Pauli antiunitary: a real structure (\(\theta^2 = 1\)) for hyperbolic quadrics and a quaternionic structure (\(\theta^2 = -1\)) for elliptic ones [J2]. \(Q_0\) is the set of real symmetric Pauli matrices (an even number of \(Y\) factors).

3. The spectral floor

Theorem 1 (spectral floor; Jonah [J2] for the three families; formalized here for arbitrary \(S\)). Let \(N \ge 1\), let \(S\) be any set of points and \(f : S \to \{\pm 1\}\). Then

\[\beta_S(f) \;\le\; \frac{(D-2)\,|S|^{3/2}}{6\sqrt{D-1}}, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad d(S) \;\ge\; \frac{L_S}{2} - \frac{(D-2)\,|S|^{3/2}}{12\sqrt{D-1}} .\]

Proof. Let \(A = \sum_{v \in S} f(v) P_v\), a Hermitian \(D \times D\) matrix.

(i) Three traces. \(\operatorname{tr} A = 0\) because \(0 \notin S\). Since \(\operatorname{tr}(P_u P_v) = D\,\delta_{uv}\) and \(f(v)^2 = 1\), \(\operatorname{tr} A^2 = D\,|S|\). Since \(\operatorname{tr}(P_u P_v P_w) = D\,\tau(u,v)\,[w = u+v]\),

\[\operatorname{tr} A^3 = D \sum_{\substack{u, v \in S \\ u+v \in S}} f(u) f(v) f(u+v)\, \tau(u,v).\]

The pairs with \(\langle u,v\rangle = 1\) cancel against their transposes, because \(\tau(v,u) = -\tau(u,v)\). The pairs with \(\langle u,v \rangle = 0\) are the ordered pairs of distinct points of a context (\(u = v\) would give \(u + v = 0 \notin S\)), six for each context, each contributing \(s_\ell f(u)f(v)f(u+v)\). Therefore \(\operatorname{tr} A^3 = 6 D\, \beta_S(f)\).

(ii) A scalar inequality (Okumura [O]). Let \(x_1, \dots, x_n\) be real with \(\sum x_i = 0\) and \(\sum x_i^2 = n m\), \(n \ge 2\), and let \(a = \sqrt{(n-1)m}\). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to the other \(n-1\) numbers, \(x_i^2 \le (n-1)(nm - x_i^2)\), so \(x_i \le a\) for every \(i\). Hence \((x_i - a)\big((n-1)x_i + a\big)^2 \le 0\). Summing over \(i\) and using the two constraints,

\[(n-1) \sum_i x_i^3 \;\le\; n\, m\, (n-2)\, a .\]

(iii) Conclusion. Apply (ii) to the eigenvalues of \(A\) with \(n = D\), \(m = |S|\): \((D-1) \cdot 6 D \beta \le D\,|S|\,(D-2)\sqrt{(D-1)|S|}\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Three remarks. First, no closure property of \(S\) is used: products that leave \(S\) do not contribute to the trace. Second, the hypothesis \(f = \pm 1\) enters only through \(\operatorname{tr} A^2 = D|S|\), so the argument bounds the cubic form \(T(x) = \sum_\ell s_\ell x_u x_v x_w\) on the sphere \(\sum x_v^2 = |S|\). Third, the bound applies to the induced configuration only, that is, with all contexts contained in \(S\).

Theorem 2 (quaternionic form; Jonah [J2]). If every \(P_v\), \(v \in S\), commutes with an antiunitary \(\theta\) with \(\theta^2 = -1\) (for instance, \(S\) lies in an elliptic quadric), then

\[\beta_S(f) \;\le\; \frac{(D-4)\,|S|^{3/2}}{6\sqrt{2(D-2)}} .\]

Proof. \(A\) is a real combination of operators commuting with \(\theta\), so \(\theta A = A \theta\). If \(A x = \lambda x\) then \(A\theta x = \lambda \theta x\), and \(\langle x, \theta x\rangle = 0\) because \(\langle \theta x, \theta y\rangle = \overline{\langle x, y \rangle}\) gives \(\langle x, \theta x\rangle = \overline{\langle \theta x, \theta^2 x\rangle} = -\langle x, \theta x\rangle\). Every eigenspace is therefore an orthogonal sum of planes \(\{x, \theta x\}\) and every eigenvalue has even multiplicity (Kramers). Apply (ii) to the \(D/2\) distinct pair values, with the same \(m\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Theorem 2 is not part of the Lean development; it is checked numerically (Section 9).

Closed forms. With the standard counts, Theorems 1 and 2 give for the three families the same expression in different arguments. Writing \(\varphi(D) = \tfrac12 - \sqrt{D+1}/(D+2)\),

4. Saturation

Theorem 3 (equality; Jonah [J2, Theorem 6]). Equality holds in Theorem 1 if and only if there is a unit vector \(\psi\) with

\[\langle \psi | P_v | \psi \rangle = f(v)\,\sqrt{\frac{D-1}{|S|}} \quad (v \in S), \qquad \langle \psi | P_v | \psi \rangle = 0 \quad (v \notin S,\ v \ne 0).\]

Proof. Equality in (ii) forces \(x_i \in \{a, -a/(n-1)\}\) for every \(i\), and the zero sum forces exactly one value \(a\). So \(A = (a-b)\,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi| + b\,I\) with \(b = -a/(D-1)\); reading off Pauli coefficients gives the claim, and the converse is the same computation backwards. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

For the full space the condition says that \(\psi\) is a fiducial vector of a SIC covariant under the \(N\)-qubit Pauli group. Such vectors exist only for \(N = 1\) and \(N = 3\) (Godsil and Roy [GR]; an elementary proof through the integrality of the floor is in [J2]): for \(N = 3\) they are the Hoggar lines.

The three saturated cases. The Hoggar fiducial \(\psi_H \propto (-1+2i, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1)\) has \(\langle P_v \rangle = \pm 1/3\) for all 63 three-qubit Pauli observables. We verified the following by exact counting in four independent implementations (Section 9); the values themselves are known [M1, dB, LGG, J2].

By Theorem 3, the optimal assignments of \(W(5,2)\) are exactly the sign patterns of Pauli-covariant SIC fiducials in dimension 8. Our enumeration finds 120 optimal violated sets in the orbit of the Hoggar pattern under \(Sp(6,2)\), and the context–point incidence matrix has rank 56 over \(\mathbb{F}_2\), so each set comes from \(2^7 = 128\) assignments: \(120 \cdot 128 = 15360 = 240 \cdot 64\) assignments, matching the 240 Hoggar-type SICs of 64 vectors each [St]. For \(Q^+(7,2)\) the orbit of our violated set under the reflections of \(O^+(8,2)\) has 240 elements, the number proved in [LGG].

Proposition 3 (violated contexts in the quantum metric). Let \(\psi\) be a state, \(p_v = \langle P_v\rangle\), and let \(Q_{uv} = \tfrac14\big(\langle P_u P_v\rangle - p_u p_v\big)\) be its quantum geometric tensor along the flows \(e^{-itP_v/2}\). For a context \(\{u, v, w\}\) with sign \(s\), the Fubini–Study metric is \(g_{uv} = \tfrac14 (s\,p_w - p_u p_v)\). If \(f = \operatorname{sign}(p)\), the context is violated exactly when \(4|g_{uv}| = |p_w| + |p_u p_v|\) and satisfied exactly when \(4|g_{uv}| = \big||p_w| - |p_u p_v|\big|\).

Proof. \(\langle P_uP_v\rangle = s\,p_w\); the context is violated when \(s\,p_w\) and \(p_u p_v\) have opposite signs. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

For \(\psi_H\): \(g_{vv} = 2/9\); \(|g_{uv}| = 1/9\) on the 189 commuting pairs lying on the 63 violated contexts, \(1/18\) on the 756 pairs lying on satisfied contexts, \(1/36\) on the 1008 anticommuting pairs; the Berry curvature has modulus \(1/6\) on anticommuting pairs and vanishes on commuting ones. The hexagon is the set of pairs of largest off-diagonal metric (computed with the QGTL kernels, Section 8).

5. Consequences for all \(N\)

Corollary A (full space). For every \(N \ge 1\) every assignment of the \(D^2 - 1\) Pauli observables violates at least \(\varphi(D)\,L\) contexts (Lean: full_space_fraction). Since a uniformly random assignment violates \(L/2\) contexts on average, \(d/L \to 1/2\).

Exponential violation. The ratio of the quantum value to the noncontextual bound of the full inequality satisfies \(L/(L-2d) \ge (D+2)/(2\sqrt{D+1}) \sim 2^{N/2-1}\). Cabello [C] proposed this inequality in 2010 and argued for exponential growth; his Lemma 3 identifies the bound with the number of positive contexts, which is correct for two qubits but not for three, where 252 of the 315 predictions can be satisfied rather than 225 [M1]. Corollary A proves the growth.

Noise. If imperfections scale every correlator by a visibility \(\eta\), the inequality is violated when \(\eta \gt 1 - 2d/L\), hence whenever \(\eta \gt 2\sqrt{D+1}/(D+2)\): above \(0.6\) for three qubits, \(0.458\) for four, \(0.338\) for five, \(0.062\) for ten. In the notation of [C, MS] the tolerated error per context \(\varepsilon = 2d/L\) is at least \(1 - 2\sqrt{D+1}/(D+2)\), which exceeds \(0.707\) (the largest value confirmed in the search of [MS], for configurations of a different kind) from six qubits on and tends to \(1\).

Proposition 4 (the floor is sharp up to a logarithmic factor). For every pure \(N\)-qubit state with \(M = \max_{v \ne 0} |\langle P_v \rangle|\) there is an assignment of the full space with

\[\beta \;\ge\; \frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{6\,M^3}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \frac{1}{(D+1)(D+2)\,M_N^3} \;\le\; \frac12 - \frac{d}{L} \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{D+1}}{D+2},\]

where \(M_N\) is the least value of \(M\) over all states. \(M_N \ge 1/\sqrt{D+1}\), with equality exactly when a Pauli-covariant SIC exists, and \(M_N \le C\sqrt{\log D / D}\) for an absolute constant \(C\).

Proof. A pure state has \(\operatorname{tr}\rho^3 = 1\); with \(\rho = D^{-1}(I + \sum_v p_v P_v)\) and \(\sum_{v\ne 0} p_v^2 = D-1\), the trace computation of Theorem 1 gives \(T(p) = \sum_\ell s_\ell\, p_u p_v p_w = (D-1)(D-2)/6\) (the cubic purity condition [JL, St]). Choose the signs \(f(v)\) independently with mean \(p_v/M\). Then \(\mathbb{E}\,\beta(f) = T(p)/M^3\), and some \(f\) does at least as well. The upper bound on \(M_N\) follows from Lévy's lemma for Haar-random states and a union bound over the \(D^2-1\) observables [HLW]. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

So \(\tfrac12 - d/L = \Theta(2^{-N/2})\) up to a factor \(\log^{3/2} D\). In four sample runs the sign patterns of Haar-random states reached between 50 and 64 per cent of the floor value of \(\beta\) for four and five qubits. We did not find Proposition 4 in the notes we could read; we do not claim it is new.

Averaging between families. Every context of \(W(7,2)\) lies on exactly 24 of its 120 elliptic quadrics, and every context of \(Q^+(9,2)\) lies in exactly 112 of its 496 sections by an imaginary Pauli observable, each a signed copy of \(W(7,2)\) [LGG, J1]; we re-verified these incidences by enumeration. With Theorem 2 and integer rounding this gives \(d(Q^-(7,2)) \ge 291\), \(d(W(7,2)) \ge 1455\), \(d(Q^+(9,2)) \ge 6444\), \(d(W(9,2)) \ge 28791\), each below the values of [J1, J4] and stated here only to show what the floor alone yields.

The main theorem, as checked:

theorem hoggar_bound (hN : 1 ≤ N) (S : Finset (Pt N)) (h0 : (0 : Pt N) ∉ S) (f : Pt N → ℝ)
    (hf : ∀ u ∈ S, f u = 1 ∨ f u = -1) :
    ((2 : ℝ) ^ N - 1) * beta6 S f
      ≤ ((2 : ℝ) ^ N - 2) * S.card * Real.sqrt (((2 : ℝ) ^ N - 1) * S.card)

Here beta6 S f is six times \(\beta_S(f)\), a sum over ordered pairs, and the sign of a context is by definition the real part of the scalar \(\tau(u,v)\) with pauli u * pauli v * pauli (u + v) = tau u v • 1. Table 2 lists the corollaries.

What is not formalized. Theorem 2 (the quaternionic form) and all elliptic-quadric numbers; Theorem 3 and every statement about equality or geometry; Proposition 4; the limit statement as a theorem about limits (the inequality for each \(N\) is formal); the exact values of [J1]. Independent audit. A separate set of agent sessions, working concurrently in the same repository and without editing our files, re-checked the six modules from source, confirmed the axiom report, and added two modules of its own (IndependentContexts.lean, IndependentWitness.lean). They prove that a violated context carries exactly six violated flags and, by kernel evaluation of an integer certificate (no native_decide), that the explicit assignment "\(f(a,c) = +1\) if \(a = 0\) or \(c = 0\) or the number of \(Y\) factors is at least 2, and \(-1\) otherwise" violates exactly 63 contexts; together with three_qubit_contexts this is a formal proof that the three-qubit degree is exactly 63. Their reports are in results/lean-audit.md, results/literature-audit.md and results/stack-independent-audit.md, and their companion note is paper/independent-research-note.md. The literature audit is what first pointed us to [J1].

8. A running example on the Tsotchke stack

All four components are used for what they are.

  • Moonlab (v1.2.1, prebuilt WebAssembly engine) is a classical state-vector simulator. It plays the quantum device.
  • quantum_rng is a classical, "quantum-inspired" integer and trigonometric mixer, not a hardware quantum source. It supplies seeded pseudo-random numbers. Its stream depends on the C library it is built against, so no result depends on its exact values: every count is recomputed independently.
  • QGTL (quantum_geometric_tensor): only the diffgeo_* kernels (Fubini–Study metric, Berry curvature), which we had verified earlier against closed forms, are used.
  • Eshkol (v1.3.6) is a Scheme dialect with automatic differentiation. It carries an independent implementation of the geometry and the differentiation checks.

9. Falsifiability and replication

Predictions that could fail. (P1) No assignment violates fewer contexts than the floor. (P2) The three saturated cases are reached exactly. (P3) For every \(S\) and \(f\), \(\operatorname{tr}A = 0\), \(\operatorname{tr}A^2 = D|S|\), \(\operatorname{tr}A^3 = 6D\beta\). (P4) On an elliptic quadric the spectrum of \(A\) is doubly degenerate. (P5) For every pure state \(T(p) = (D-1)(D-2)/6\). (P6) Ideal sequential measurements reproduce every context sign in every state. A single counterexample to P1 would be an assignment whose violation count anyone can recompute in milliseconds.

Tests (scripts/run_all.sh).

  • hoggar exhaust2: all 32768 two-qubit assignments; minimum 3 (640 assignments); for the square, minimum 1.
  • scripts/run_falsify.sh: 20 runs over \(N = 2, \dots, 5\) and the three families; 20000, 5000 and 500 uniformly random assignments and 1500, 200 and 12 annealed ones per configuration for \(N = 3, 4, 5\). No assignment below the floor. Minima reached by annealing: 63, 21, 9 (\(N=3\)); 1575, 315, 315 (\(N=4\)); 31479, 6975, 7087 (\(N=5\)). These equal the best known values in every case.
  • A negative control: the false claim \(d(W(5,2)) \ge 64\) is refuted by the Hoggar witness with exit code 3, showing that the harness can fail.
  • scripts/analysis.py (62 checks) and scripts/ladder.py (14 checks): P3 on random data to machine precision; P4 on all 120 four-qubit elliptic quadrics; P5; the geometry of the violated sets; closed-form counts against enumeration; agreement of the C and Python integer bounds.
  • Witness files (data/witness/) are recounted by an independent routine that uses no tables.

Independent implementations. The geometry, the context signs and the counts are implemented five times: C (src/hoggar.c), Python/NumPy with explicit matrices (scripts/analysis.py), JavaScript (src/moonlab_experiment.mjs), Eshkol, and the Lean model, in which the signs are theorems about matrix products. They agree on every number in this paper.

10. Limitations and open problems

  • The floor is not attained beyond the three listed cases [J2]. Exact values for five or more qubits are open except \(Q^+(9,2)\) [J1]; the floor alone leaves the intervals of Table 1.
  • The exact four- and five-qubit values rest on computer-assisted work that is neither formally verified nor peer reviewed [J1]. A formal proof of \(d(W(7,2)) = 1575\) would be a natural next target; our Lean development gives the lower bound 1451 only.
  • The quaternionic form (Theorem 2) is proved on paper and tested numerically, not formalized.
  • The literature search was bounded (arXiv and Zenodo queries on 9 November 2026). Statements of the form "to our knowledge" mean exactly that.
  • Nothing here is an experiment on quantum hardware, and nothing shows a computational advantage. The simulation demonstrates what quantum mechanics predicts and what no noncontextual model can do.
