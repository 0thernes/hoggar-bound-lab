A machine-checked spectral floor for Pauli contextuality, and new configurations that attain it

Lean 4 formalization for every number of qubits; exact contextuality degrees of four-, five- and seven-qubit configurations outside the classical families; a classification of flat third-level phase states; a running demonstration on the Tsotchke stack

Abstract

A noncontextual hidden-variable model assigns a value \(\pm 1\) to every \(N\)-qubit Pauli observable. The contextuality degree of a set of observables is the least number of its contexts (commuting triples with product \(\pm I\)) that such an assignment must get wrong. We give a kernel-checked Lean 4 proof, for every \(N\) and for arbitrary sets \(S\), of the spectral floor first posted by B. W. Jonah: the number of satisfied minus violated contexts is at most \((D-2)\,|S|^{3/2} / (6\sqrt{D-1})\), where \(D = 2^N\). The floor is attained exactly when a pure state has Pauli expectation values of one modulus on \(S\) and zero elsewhere. We exhibit such configurations outside the classical families and determine their degrees exactly: 135 four-qubit observables with 1095 contexts (degree 75) and 496 five-qubit observables with 10880 contexts (degree 480), both proved in Lean with kernel-evaluated counts, and 8128 seven-qubit observables with 2 824 192 contexts (degree 729 344). The Pauli support of any such state is a two-character set, hence gives a strongly regular graph, and a bent function in the three-, five- and seven-qubit cases. By exhaustive enumeration we classify the states \(U|{+}\cdots{+}\rangle\), with \(U\) a diagonal gate of the third level of the Clifford hierarchy, that have this property on up to five qubits: there is none on four qubits and one class on five, a circuit of three controlled-S and two \(CCZ\) gates whose configuration is Clifford-equivalent to the support of a state found numerically by Cieśliński. On seven qubits a circuit of three controlled-S and eight \(CCZ\) gates gives the configuration above. For the classical families the checked corollaries include a violated fraction of at least \(1/2 - \sqrt{D+1}/(D+2)\) for the full \(N\)-qubit configuration, for every \(N\). The work was produced with an AI research system under the author's direction, as disclosed in the paper.

1. Scope

1.1 What is new in this work

  1. A formal proof of the floor. The spectral floor is proved in Lean 4 for every number of qubits \(N \ge 1\) and for arbitrary sets of observables with their induced contexts (theorem hoggar_bound). The proof is about the actual matrices: Pauli operators are defined as explicit complex matrices indexed by bit vectors, their product phases and commutation rule are theorems, and the bound is derived from the spectral theorem. Earlier accounts of the bound are explicitly not formally verified [J2], and the earlier Lean development of Li, Gupta and Ganesh [LGG] covers one four-qubit configuration by a different (combinatorial) argument.
  2. Exact degrees outside the classical families (Section 5). The published statements of the floor and of its equality case concern families closed under products of commuting pairs: the full space and the quadrics [J2]. The trace argument needs no closure, and the floor is attained on sets that are not closed. We determine two such degrees exactly: 75 for a 135-point four-qubit configuration with 1095 contexts, and 480 for a 496-point five-qubit configuration with 10880 contexts. Both are proved in Lean in full (theorems pivot_degree and cieslinski5_degree): the lower bound is an instance of hoggar_bound, the upper bound an explicit assignment, and the counts are kernel evaluations.
  3. A four-qubit state. The state \(\psi_B\) of the abstract has Pauli expectation values \(\pm 1/3\) on 135 observables and \(0\) on the other 120, like the known state \((-3,1,\dots,1)/\sqrt{24}\), but its support is not a quadric. We classify all \(4^6\) states with fourth-root-of-unity amplitudes on the same six basis states: they fall into exactly two classes, according to the parity of the number of imaginary amplitudes (Proposition 6).
  4. A saturation criterion for arbitrary sets (Theorem 4), in spectral, state and purely combinatorial form; the observation that for every such state the violated contexts are the pairs of largest quantum metric (Proposition 8); and the fact that the support of such a state is a two-character set, so that it defines a strongly regular graph, and a bent function when the modulus is \(2^{-(N-1)/2}\) (Proposition 10).
  5. Flat phase states (Section 5.3). A state \(U|{+}\cdots{+}\rangle\) with \(U\) a product of \(T\), controlled-S (\(CS\)) and \(CCZ\) gates has a single non-zero modulus exactly when a symmetric trilinear form over \(\mathbb{F}_2\) has constant rank (Proposition 11). We enumerate all such forms on up to five qubits (Theorem 12): none on four qubits, one class on five. The five-qubit class gives a five-gate circuit for a state with the Pauli support of [Ci]'s numerically found \(s_5\), up to a Clifford operation.
  6. Seven qubits (Theorem 13). A circuit of three \(CS\) and eight \(CCZ\) gates prepares a state with expectation values \(\pm 1/8\) on 8128 observables and \(0\) on the others. The configuration has 2 824 192 contexts and contextuality degree exactly 729 344. Within the family of forms we could enumerate completely (about \(1.5 \times 10^{10}\) forms) it is unique up to CNOT circuits.
  7. Formally verified numerical corollaries for the classical families, listed in the abstract and in Table 2, including formal answers to two questions asked in [LGG]: the degree of \(Q^+(9,2)\) exceeds \(4743\), and the violated fraction of the four-qubit full space and of the five-qubit hyperbolic quadric is strictly above \(1/5\). The exact values 63 (three qubits) and 315 (four-qubit hyperbolic quadric) are also proved in full, by the spectral route.
  8. Consequences spelled out for all \(N\): an exponentially growing quantum-to-classical ratio with a proof (the 2010 argument for such growth rests on a lemma that fails at three qubits), a noise threshold that tends to zero, and a two-sided estimate of the optimal violated fraction (Proposition 4).
  9. An independent reproduction and a running demonstration: a search engine, four independent implementations of the geometry (C, Python, JavaScript, Eshkol) and the Lean model agree on every count; the experiments run on the Tsotchke stack with each component labelled for what it is. The five- and seven-qubit states are prepared there as circuits.

2. Setting

Pauli operators. Let \(D = 2^N\). A point is a nonzero vector \(v = (a \mid c) \in \mathbb{F}_2^{2N}\). It labels the Hermitian operator

\[P_v \;=\; i^{\,|a \wedge c|}\, X^{a} Z^{c},\]

a tensor product of \(I, X, Y, Z\) (the exponent \(|a \wedge c|\) counts the factors equal to \(Y = iXZ\)). \(P_0 = I\), \(P_v^2 = I\), and \(\operatorname{tr} P_v = 0\) for \(v \ne 0\). Products close up to a phase,

\[P_u P_v = \tau(u,v)\, P_{u+v}, \qquad \tau(u,v) = i^{\,k_u + k_v + 3 k_{u+v}}\,(-1)^{c_u \cdot a_v}, \quad k_v = |a_v \wedge c_v| ,\]

and with the symplectic form \(\langle u, v\rangle = c_u\cdot a_v + c_v \cdot a_u\) one has \(\tau(v,u) = (-1)^{\langle u,v\rangle}\tau(u,v)\) and \(\tau(u,v)^2 = (-1)^{\langle u,v\rangle}\). So \(P_u\) and \(P_v\) commute exactly when \(\langle u,v\rangle = 0\), and then \(\tau(u,v) = s(u,v) \in \{+1,-1\}\).

Contexts. A context (a line of the symplectic polar space \(W(2N-1,2)\)) is a set \(\ell = \{u, v, u+v\}\) of three distinct points with \(\langle u, v\rangle = 0\). Its three operators commute and \(P_u P_v P_{u+v} = s_\ell\, I\) with \(s_\ell = s(u,v)\), which does not depend on the order.

Configurations and assignments. Fix a set \(S\) of points and let \(\mathcal{L}(S)\) be the set of all contexts contained in \(S\), \(L_S = |\mathcal{L}(S)|\). \(S\) is closed if the product of any two commuting observables of \(S\) is again in \(S\). An assignment \(f : S \to \{+1,-1\}\) violates \(\ell\) when \(f(u) f(v) f(u+v) \neq s_\ell\). Put

\[\beta_S(f) = \sum_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}(S)} s_\ell\, f(u) f(v) f(u+v) = L_S - 2\,\mathrm{viol}(f), \qquad d(S) = \min_f \mathrm{viol}(f).\]

\(d(S)\) is the contextuality degree [dB]. For the noncontextuality inequality \(\chi = \sum_\ell s_\ell \langle P_u P_v P_{u+v}\rangle \le L_S - 2 d(S)\) quantum mechanics gives \(\chi = L_S\) in every state [C, LGG].

Three families. The full space is \(S = \mathbb{F}_2^{2N}\setminus\{0\}\), with \(D^2-1\) points and \(L = (D^2-1)(D^2-4)/12\) contexts. For \(c \in \mathbb{F}_2^{2N}\) let \(q_0(v) = |a\wedge c| \bmod 2\) and \(Q_c = \{ v \ne 0 : q_0(v) + \langle c, v\rangle = 0\}\). \(Q_c\) is a hyperbolic quadric \(Q^+(2N-1,2)\) if \(q_0(c) = 0\) and an elliptic quadric \(Q^-(2N-1,2)\) if \(q_0(c) = 1\). With \(K\) complex conjugation in the computational basis, \(\theta_c = P_c K\) is antiunitary, \(\theta_c P_v \theta_c^{-1} = (-1)^{q_0(v) + \langle c,v\rangle} P_v\) and \(\theta_c^2 = (-1)^{q_0(c)}\). So the quadrics are the sets of Pauli observables fixed by a Pauli antiunitary: a real structure (\(\theta^2 = 1\)) for hyperbolic quadrics and a quaternionic structure (\(\theta^2 = -1\)) for elliptic ones [J2]. \(Q_0\) is the set of real symmetric Pauli matrices (an even number of \(Y\) factors). All three families are closed.

3. The spectral floor

Theorem 1 (spectral floor; Jonah [J2] for the three families; formalized here for arbitrary \(S\)). Let \(N \ge 1\), let \(S\) be any set of points and \(f : S \to \{\pm 1\}\). Then

\[\beta_S(f) \;\le\; \frac{(D-2)\,|S|^{3/2}}{6\sqrt{D-1}}, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad d(S) \;\ge\; \frac{L_S}{2} - \frac{(D-2)\,|S|^{3/2}}{12\sqrt{D-1}} .\]

Proof. Let \(A = \sum_{v \in S} f(v) P_v\), a Hermitian \(D \times D\) matrix.

(i) Three traces. \(\operatorname{tr} A = 0\) because \(0 \notin S\). Since \(\operatorname{tr}(P_u P_v) = D\,\delta_{uv}\) and \(f(v)^2 = 1\), \(\operatorname{tr} A^2 = D\,|S|\). Since \(\operatorname{tr}(P_u P_v P_w) = D\,\tau(u,v)\,[w = u+v]\),

\[\operatorname{tr} A^3 = D \sum_{\substack{u, v \in S \\ u+v \in S}} f(u) f(v) f(u+v)\, \tau(u,v).\]

The pairs with \(\langle u,v\rangle = 1\) cancel against their transposes, because \(\tau(v,u) = -\tau(u,v)\). The pairs with \(\langle u,v \rangle = 0\) are the ordered pairs of distinct points of a context (\(u = v\) would give \(u + v = 0 \notin S\)), six for each context, each contributing \(s_\ell f(u)f(v)f(u+v)\). Therefore \(\operatorname{tr} A^3 = 6 D\, \beta_S(f)\).

(ii) A scalar inequality (Okumura [O]). Let \(x_1, \dots, x_n\) be real with \(\sum x_i = 0\) and \(\sum x_i^2 = n m\), \(n \ge 2\), and let \(a = \sqrt{(n-1)m}\). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to the other \(n-1\) numbers, \(x_i^2 \le (n-1)(nm - x_i^2)\), so \(x_i \le a\) for every \(i\). Hence \((x_i - a)\big((n-1)x_i + a\big)^2 \le 0\). Summing over \(i\) and using the two constraints,

\[(n-1) \sum_i x_i^3 \;\le\; n\, m\, (n-2)\, a .\]

(iii) Conclusion. Apply (ii) to the eigenvalues of \(A\) with \(n = D\), \(m = |S|\): \((D-1) \cdot 6 D \beta \le D\,|S|\,(D-2)\sqrt{(D-1)|S|}\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Three remarks. First, no closure property of \(S\) is used: products that leave \(S\) do not contribute to the trace. Second, the hypothesis \(f = \pm 1\) enters only through \(\operatorname{tr} A^2 = D|S|\), so the argument bounds the cubic form \(T(x) = \sum_\ell s_\ell x_u x_v x_w\) on the sphere \(\sum x_v^2 = |S|\). Third, the bound applies to the induced configuration only, that is, with all contexts contained in \(S\).

Theorem 2 (quaternionic form; Jonah [J2]). If every \(P_v\), \(v \in S\), commutes with an antiunitary \(\theta\) with \(\theta^2 = -1\) (for instance, \(S\) lies in an elliptic quadric), then

\[\beta_S(f) \;\le\; \frac{(D-4)\,|S|^{3/2}}{6\sqrt{2(D-2)}} .\]

Proof. \(A\) is a real combination of operators commuting with \(\theta\), so \(\theta A = A \theta\). If \(A x = \lambda x\) then \(A\theta x = \lambda \theta x\), and \(\langle x, \theta x\rangle = 0\) because \(\langle \theta x, \theta y\rangle = \overline{\langle x, y \rangle}\) gives \(\langle x, \theta x\rangle = \overline{\langle \theta x, \theta^2 x\rangle} = -\langle x, \theta x\rangle\). Every eigenspace is therefore an orthogonal sum of planes \(\{x, \theta x\}\) and every eigenvalue has even multiplicity (Kramers). Apply (ii) to the \(D/2\) distinct pair values, with the same \(m\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Theorem 2 is not part of the Lean development; it is checked numerically (Section 10).

4. Saturation in the classical families

Theorem 3 (equality; Jonah [J2, Theorem 6] for the three families). Equality holds in Theorem 1 if and only if there is a unit vector \(\psi\) with

\[\langle \psi | P_v | \psi \rangle = f(v)\,\sqrt{\frac{D-1}{|S|}} \quad (v \in S), \qquad \langle \psi | P_v | \psi \rangle = 0 \quad (v \notin S,\ v \ne 0).\]

Proof. Equality in (ii) forces \(x_i \in \{a, -a/(n-1)\}\) for every \(i\), and the zero sum forces exactly one value \(a\). So \(A = (a-b)\,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi| + b\,I\) with \(b = -a/(D-1)\); reading off Pauli coefficients gives the claim, and the converse is the same computation backwards. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

We call such a \(\psi\) flat on \(S\), after [Ci]. For the full space the condition says that \(\psi\) is a fiducial vector of a SIC covariant under the \(N\)-qubit Pauli group. Such vectors exist only for \(N = 1\) and \(N = 3\) (Godsil and Roy [GR]; an elementary proof through the integrality of the floor is in [J2]): for \(N = 3\) they are the Hoggar lines.

Proposition 3 (violated contexts in the quantum metric). Let \(\psi\) be a state, \(p_v = \langle P_v\rangle\), and let \(Q_{uv} = \tfrac14\big(\langle P_u P_v\rangle - p_u p_v\big)\) be its quantum geometric tensor along the flows \(e^{-itP_v/2}\). For a context \(\{u, v, w\}\) with sign \(s\), the Fubini–Study metric is \(g_{uv} = \tfrac14 (s\,p_w - p_u p_v)\). If \(f = \operatorname{sign}(p)\), the context is violated exactly when \(4|g_{uv}| = |p_w| + |p_u p_v|\) and satisfied exactly when \(4|g_{uv}| = \big||p_w| - |p_u p_v|\big|\).

Proof. \(\langle P_uP_v\rangle = s\,p_w\); the context is violated when \(s\,p_w\) and \(p_u p_v\) have opposite signs. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

For \(\psi_H\): \(g_{vv} = 2/9\); \(|g_{uv}| = 1/9\) on the 189 commuting pairs lying on the 63 violated contexts, \(1/18\) on the 756 pairs lying on satisfied contexts, \(1/36\) on the 1008 anticommuting pairs; the Berry curvature has modulus \(1/6\) on anticommuting pairs and vanishes on commuting ones. The hexagon is the set of pairs of largest off-diagonal metric (computed with the QGTL kernels, Section 9).

5. Saturation outside the classical families

5.1 The criterion

The statements of this section concern arbitrary sets \(S\), which need not be closed. For an assignment \(f\) and a point \(w \in S\) let the excess \(e_w(f)\) be the number of satisfied minus the number of violated contexts of \(S\) through \(w\). For a point \(w \notin S\), \(w \ne 0\), let the outside sum be \(b_w(f) = \sum s(u,v) f(u) f(v)\) over the unordered pairs of commuting points \(u, v\) of \(S\) with \(u + v = w\).

Theorem 4 (saturation criterion). Let \(S\) be a set of points, \(f : S \to \{\pm1\}\) and \(A = \sum_{v\in S} f(v) P_v\). Then

\[A^2 \;=\; |S|\, I \;+\; 2 \sum_{w \in S} e_w(f)\, f(w)\, P_w \;+\; 2 \sum_{w \notin S,\ w \neq 0} b_w(f)\, P_w ,\]

and the following are equivalent:

  • (a) \(f\) attains the floor: \(\beta_S(f) = (D-2)|S|^{3/2}/(6\sqrt{D-1})\);
  • (b) \(A\) has the simple eigenvalue \(\sqrt{(D-1)|S|}\) and the eigenvalue \(-\sqrt{|S|/(D-1)}\) with multiplicity \(D-1\);
  • (c) some pure state is flat on \(S\) with signs \(f\) (Theorem 3);
  • (d) \(e_w(f) = (D-2)\sqrt{|S|}\,/\,(2\sqrt{D-1})\) for every \(w \in S\), and \(b_w(f) = 0\) for every \(w \notin S\).

When they hold, \(d(S) = \tfrac12 L_S - (D-2)|S|^{3/2}/(12\sqrt{D-1})\) exactly, \(f\) is optimal, and, for \(N \ge 2\), \((D-1)|S|\) is a perfect square.

Proof. In \(A^2 = \sum_{u,v} f(u)f(v)P_uP_v\) the diagonal terms give \(|S|\,I\) and the two orders of an anticommuting pair cancel. A commuting pair \(u \ne v\) contributes \(2 s(u,v) f(u) f(v) P_{u+v}\). If \(w = u+v \in S\), the pair is a context through \(w\) and \(s(u,v)f(u)f(v) = \pm f(w)\) according to whether it is satisfied; summing over the contexts through \(w\) gives \(2 e_w(f) f(w) P_w\). If \(w \notin S\) the pairs give \(2 b_w(f) P_w\). This is the identity; for closed \(S\) the last sum is empty and it is Theorem 1 of [J2]. (a) \(\Leftrightarrow\) (b) is the equality case of the scalar inequality, and (b) \(\Leftrightarrow\) (c) is Theorem 3. Condition (b) says \((A - a)(A + a/(D-1)) = 0\) with \(a = \sqrt{(D-1)|S|}\), that is \(A^2 = |S|\,I + \tfrac{a(D-2)}{D-1} A\) (a traceless matrix with these two eigenvalues has the larger one exactly once); comparing Pauli coefficients with the identity gives (d). The floor is an integer when attained, which for \(D \gt 2\) forces \(\sqrt{(D-1)|S|}\) to be rational. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Condition (d) replaces the violation-regularity of [J2, Theorem 6], to which it reduces when every point lies on the same number of contexts. For \(N \le 5\) the number \(D - 1\) is squarefree, so a set that attains the floor has \(|S| = (D-1)k^2\) and excess \((D-2)k/2\): \(|S| \in \{3, 12\}\) for two qubits, \(\{7, 28, 63\}\) for three, \(\{15, 60, 135, 240\}\) for four, \(\{31, 124, 279, 496, 775\}\) for five.

5.2 A four-qubit and a five-qubit configuration

Theorem 5 (the pivot configuration). Let

\[\psi_B \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\,|0011\rangle + |0111\rangle + |1011\rangle + |1100\rangle + |1101\rangle + i\,|1110\rangle\,\big),\]

with qubit 0 written on the right, and let \(S_B\) be the set of observables with non-zero expectation value in \(\psi_B\).

  1. The expectation values are \(\pm 1/3\) on \(S_B\), and \(|S_B| = 135\): the 15 diagonal observables \((0 \mid c)\), and for each \(a \ne 0\) the eight observables \((a \mid c)\) with \(a \cdot c = \epsilon(a)\), where \(\epsilon(a) = 1\) for the five values \(a \in E = \{0010, 0011, 0101, 1001, 1101\}\) and \(\epsilon(a) = 0\) otherwise.
  2. \(S_B\) contains 1095 contexts: 35 through each diagonal observable and 23 through each of the other 120. It is not closed and it is not a quadric, but every hyperplane \(w^\perp\) meets it in 71 points if \(w \in S_B\) and in 63 points otherwise, as for \(Q^+(7,2)\): it is a quasi-quadric in the sense of [DHOP]. It differs from the quadric \(a \cdot c = 0\) in 80 points, those with \(a \in E\), and \(E\) consists of five pairwise non-orthogonal vectors (an ovoid) of the doily \(W(3,2)\) defined by the form \(x_0y_1 + x_1y_0 + x_2y_3 + x_3y_2\).
  3. \(d(S_B) = 75\). The assignment given by the signs of the expectation values violates the 15 lines of that doily, read among the diagonal observables, and 60 further contexts, each with exactly one diagonal observable. Every non-diagonal observable of \(S_B\) lies on exactly one violated context and every diagonal one on seven.

Proof. Items 1 and 2 and the count of 75 violated contexts are finite computations in \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) (Section 10); item 1 also follows from Proposition 6. The bound \(d(S_B) \ge 75\) is Theorem 1 with \(|S| = 135\), \(D = 16\), \(L = 1095\): \(\beta \le 14 \cdot 135 \cdot 45 / (6 \cdot 15) = 945\). In Lean this is pivot_degree. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

The noncontextuality inequality of this configuration has classical bound \(945\) and quantum value \(1095\) in every state. The quadric \(Q^+(7,2)\) has the same number of observables and the same classical bound, with quantum value \(1575\).

Proposition 6 (flat states on a bent support). Let \(T\) be the set of the six \(x \in \mathbb{F}_2^4\) with \(x_0x_1 + x_2x_3 = 1\), the support of a bent function, and let \(\phi : T \to \mathbb{C}\) with \(|\phi| = 1\). The state \(\psi_\phi = 6^{-1/2}\sum_{x \in T}\phi(x)|x\rangle\) is flat on its Pauli support if and only if, up to a global phase, all \(\phi(x)\) are fourth roots of unity. In that case the support has 135 points and the modulus is \(1/3\); it is a hyperbolic quadric (1575 contexts, degree 315) when the number of imaginary amplitudes is even, and it has 1095 contexts and degree 75 when that number is odd. The two classes are not related by any Clifford operation.

Proof. \(T\) is a difference set with parameters \((16,6,2)\) [R]: every non-trivial character sum over \(T\) is \(\pm 2\), and for every \(a \ne 0\) exactly one unordered pair \(x_a, x_a + a\) lies in \(T\). Hence \(\langle Z^c\rangle = \pm 1/3\) for \(c \ne 0\), and for \(a \ne 0\), with \(z_a = \overline{\phi(x_a + a)}\,\phi(x_a)\),

\[\langle X^a Z^c \rangle = \tfrac16 (-1)^{c \cdot x_a}\big( z_a + (-1)^{c \cdot a}\, \overline{z_a} \big),\]

so the expectation value of \(P_{(a|c)}\) is \(\pm\tfrac13 \operatorname{Re} z_a\) when \(a \cdot c = 0\) and \(\pm\tfrac13 \operatorname{Im} z_a\) when \(a \cdot c = 1\). A common modulus must be \(1/3\), which holds exactly when every \(z_a\) is \(\pm 1\) or \(\pm i\), that is, when all ratios of amplitudes are fourth roots of unity. Then for each \(a \ne 0\) exactly the eight observables with \(a \cdot c = \epsilon(a)\) are in the support, where \(\epsilon(a) = 0\) if \(z_a\) is real and \(1\) if it is imaginary. Write \(\phi = i^{k}\) and \(\kappa = k \bmod 2\); then \(\epsilon(x + x') = \kappa_x + \kappa_{x'}\). A diagonal Clifford operation multiplies \(\phi\) by \(i^{q(x)}\) for a \(\mathbb{Z}_4\)-valued quadratic form \(q\) and changes \(\kappa\) by the restriction to \(T\) of a linear functional; these sixteen restrictions have weights 0, 2 and 4, and together with the all-ones vector (a global factor \(i\)) they span the even-weight vectors of \(\mathbb{F}_2^{T}\). So \(\kappa\) of even weight is equivalent to \(\kappa = 0\), a real state, whose support is the quadric \(a \cdot c = 0\); and \(\kappa\) of odd weight is equivalent to a single imaginary amplitude, as in \(\psi_B\). Symplectic maps preserve the number of contexts, and \(1575 \ne 1095\). The two counts were computed for all 32 supports that occur, over all 4096 phase patterns (Section 10). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

The lower bound is Theorem 1 with \(|S| = 496\), \(D = 32\): \(\beta \le 30 \cdot 496 \cdot 124/(6 \cdot 31) = 9920 = 10880 - 2\cdot 480\). In Lean this is cieslinski5_degree. Proposition 8 (the optimum in the quantum metric). Let \(\psi\) be flat on \(S\) with modulus \(\mu\), and let \(f\) be the signs of its expectation values. For \(u \ne v\) in \(S\) the Fubini–Study metric of Proposition 3 has \(|g_{uv}| = (\mu + \mu^2)/4\) if \(u, v, u+v\) form a context of \(S\) violated by \(f\), \(|g_{uv}| = (\mu - \mu^2)/4\) if they form a satisfied context of \(S\), and \(|g_{uv}| = \mu^2/4\) otherwise (\(u\), \(v\) anticommute, or their product is outside \(S\)). So the violated contexts of the optimal assignment are exactly the pairs with \(4|g_{uv}|\) larger than \(\mu\). The Berry curvature has modulus \(\mu/2\) on anticommuting pairs whose product is in \(S\) and vanishes on all other pairs.

Proof. Proposition 3 with \(|p| = \mu\) on \(S\) and \(p = 0\) outside; for anticommuting \(u, v\) the product \(P_uP_v\) is \(\pm i P_{u+v}\), so \(\langle P_uP_v\rangle\) is imaginary, of modulus \(|p_{u+v}|\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Proposition 9 (two mutually unbiased stabilizer states). Let \(s_1, s_2\) be stabilizer state vectors whose stabilizer groups meet only in the identity, so that \(|\langle s_1|s_2\rangle| = D^{-1/2}\), and let \(\varphi = \theta + \arg\langle s_1|s_2\rangle\). The normalized state proportional to \(s_1 + e^{i\theta}s_2\) is flat on a set that is not a stabilizer group exactly in the following cases: \(N = 1\) with \(\cos\varphi = (\sqrt6 - \sqrt2)/4\) or \(-(\sqrt6 + \sqrt2)/4\) (all 3 observables, modulus \(1/\sqrt3\)); \(N = 2\) with \(\varphi = \pm\pi/2\) (12 observables, modulus \(1/2\)); \(N = 3\) with \(\varphi = \pm 3\pi/4\) (all 63, modulus \(1/3\): the Hoggar fiducial is \(\sqrt8\) times the uniform vector plus \((-2+2i)\) times the first basis vector); \(N = 4\) with \(\varphi = \pi\) (the 135 observables of a hyperbolic quadric, modulus \(1/3\): \((-3,1,\dots,1)\) is 4 times the uniform unit vector minus 4 times the first basis vector). There is none for \(N \ge 5\).

Proof. Let \(t = D^{-1/2}\) and \(n = 2 + 2t\cos\varphi\) the squared norm. An observable of the first stabilizer group has expectation \(\pm(1 + 2t\cos\varphi)/n\), and so has one of the second. Every other observable is, up to a phase, a product \(P_1P_2\) of non-identity elements of the two groups, and its expectation is \(\pm 2t\cos\varphi/n\) if \(P_1, P_2\) commute and \(\pm 2t\sin\varphi/n\) if they anticommute. Equating the non-zero moduli: all three are equal only if \(|\cos\varphi| = |\sin\varphi|\) and \(1 - \sqrt2\,t = \sqrt2\,t\), so \(D = 8\); with \(\sin\varphi = 0\) one needs \(1 - 2t = 2t\), so \(D = 16\) and \(\cos\varphi = -1\); with \(\cos\varphi = 0\) one needs \(1 = 2t\), so \(D = 4\); for \(D = 2\) there are no commuting products and \(|1 + \sqrt2\cos\varphi| = \sqrt2|\sin\varphi|\) has the two stated solutions. The remaining case \(1 + 2t\cos\varphi = 0\) gives a stabilizer state. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

So the SIC fiducials of one and three qubits, a state with the Pauli spectrum of [LLY], and the quadric state are four members of one family, and that family stops at four qubits. The pivot state is not in it: it is a combination of three pairwise unbiased stabilizer states.

5.3 Flat phase states, and a seven-qubit configuration

Proposition 10 (the support is a two-character set). Let \(\psi\) be flat on \(S\) and let \(k^2 = |S|/(D-1)\), the inverse square of the modulus. For every point \(w\),

\[\sum_{v \in S} (-1)^{\langle v, w\rangle} \;=\; D\,[w \in S] \;-\; k^2 .\]

Equivalently, exactly \((D-2)|S|/(2(D-1)) + \tfrac{D}{2}[w \in S]\) observables of \(S\) commute with \(P_w\). Hence the graph on \(\mathbb{F}_2^{2N}\) in which \(u\) and \(v\) are adjacent when \(u + v \in S\) is strongly regular (or complete), with eigenvalues \(|S|\), \(D - k^2\) and \(-k^2\) [CK]. If \(k^2 = D/2\) the sum takes the values \(\pm D/2\): then \(S\) is a Hadamard difference set, the support of a bent function on the \(2N\)-dimensional space.

Proof. For a pure state \(\rho\) and any \(w\), \(\operatorname{tr}(\rho P_w \rho P_w) = \langle P_w\rangle^2\). Writing \(\rho = D^{-1}\sum_v p_v P_v\) with \(p_0 = 1\) and using \(P_wP_vP_w = (-1)^{\langle v,w\rangle}P_v\) gives \(p_w^2 = D^{-1}\sum_v (-1)^{\langle v,w\rangle}p_v^2\). Insert \(p_v^2 = k^{-2}\) on \(S\), \(1\) at \(v = 0\) and \(0\) elsewhere. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

The identity for \(p_w^2\) holds for every pure state and is standard; we did not find its consequence for flat states stated. It accounts for the hyperplane numbers in Theorems 5 and 7. For the two four-qubit rows of Table 3 it gives a strongly regular graph with the parameters \((256, 135, 70, 72)\) of the affine polar graph of \(Q^+(7,2)\); for the three-, five- and seven-qubit rows with modulus \(2^{-(N-1)/2}\) it gives bent functions in 6, 10 and 14 variables.

Phase states. Let \(a_j \in \mathbb{Z}_8\), \(b_{jk} \in \mathbb{Z}_4\), \(c_{jkl} \in \mathbb{Z}_2\) and

\[p(x) = \sum_j a_j x_j + 2\sum_{j \lt k} b_{jk}\, x_j x_k + 4\sum_{j \lt k \lt l} c_{jkl}\, x_j x_k x_l \pmod 8, \qquad |\Phi_p\rangle = D^{-1/2}\sum_{x} e^{i\pi p(x)/4}\,|x\rangle .\]

\(\Phi_p\) is obtained from \(|{+}\cdots{+}\rangle\) by \(T\) gates (\(a_j\)), controlled-S gates \(CS\) (\(b_{jk}\)) and \(CCZ\) gates (\(c_{jkl}\)); these are the diagonal gates of the third level of the Clifford hierarchy [CGK]. Let \(T\) be the symmetric trilinear form on \(\mathbb{F}_2^N\) with \(T(e_j,e_j,e_j) = a_j\), \(T(e_j,e_j,e_k) = T(e_j,e_k,e_k) = b_{jk}\) and \(T(e_j,e_k,e_l) = c_{jkl}\) modulo 2. Then \(B(x,z) = T(x,x,z)\) is a symmetric bilinear form, alternating exactly when there is no \(T\) gate. A linear change of variables in \(T\) is a CNOT circuit on the state, and two polynomials with the same form differ by a diagonal Clifford gate.

Then the modulus is \(2^{-r/2}\), the support has \((D-1)2^r\) points, \(r\) is even if \(N \ge 2\), and \(\Phi_p\) attains the floor on its support. \[S = \{\,(a \mid c) \;:\; a \ne 0,\ \ c\cdot\rho(a) = \epsilon(a)\,\}\]

for a function \(\epsilon\): the support of a bent function of Maiorana–McFarland type [McF].

Proof. Modulo 8 the third difference of \(p\) is \(4T\), independent of the base point, and the second difference is even. So \(p(x) - p(x+a) = h_a + 2q_a(x)\) with \(q_a(0) = 0\) and \(q_a(x+y) = q_a(x) + q_a(y) + 2T(a,x,y)\) modulo 4, a \(\mathbb{Z}_4\)-valued quadratic form [CCKS], and \(\langle X^aZ^c\rangle = D^{-1}e^{i\pi h_a/4}\sum_x i^{q_a(x)}(-1)^{c\cdot x}\). Now

\[\Big|\sum_x i^{q_a(x)}(-1)^{c\cdot x}\Big|^2 = \sum_z i^{q_a(z)}(-1)^{c\cdot z}\sum_y (-1)^{T(a,y,z)} = D \sum_{z \in R_a} i^{q_a(z)}(-1)^{c\cdot z} .\]

On \(R_a\) the map \(z \mapsto i^{q_a(z)}\) is a character with values \(\pm1\), so the last sum is \(2^{N-r_a}\) when \((-1)^{c\cdot z}\) agrees with it on \(R_a\) and \(0\) otherwise. That \(r\) is even follows from Theorem 4. If \(r = N-1\) then \(T(a,\rho(a),\cdot) = 0\), which is symmetric in \(a\) and \(\rho(a)\), so \(\rho(\rho(a)) = a\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

5.4 What does not attain the floor

For the \(D^2 - D\) observables outside a maximal commuting set (the non-diagonal ones, say) there are \((D-1)(D-2)D^2/12\) contexts and Theorem 1 gives a violated fraction of at least \(\tfrac12 - 2^{-N/2}\). By Theorem 4, equality would need a state with all non-diagonal expectation values of modulus \(2^{-N/2}\), that is, a state whose orbit under the Pauli group forms, with the computational basis, a complete set of mutually unbiased bases. For two qubits this is the first row of Table 3. For four qubits and for every odd \(N \ge 3\) no such state exists [K], so the degree is strictly above the floor there. A numerical search from random starts found flat four-qubit states only with \(|S| = 135\) (both classes of Proposition 6) and flat five-qubit states only with \(|S| = 496\); it found none with \(|S| = 60\), or with \(|S|\) equal to 124, 279 or 775. This is evidence, not proof, that Table 3 is complete up to five qubits.

6. Consequences for all \(N\)

Corollary A (full space). For every \(N \ge 1\) every assignment of the \(D^2 - 1\) Pauli observables violates at least \(\varphi(D)\,L\) contexts (Lean: full_space_fraction). Since a uniformly random assignment violates \(L/2\) contexts on average, \(d/L \to 1/2\).

Exponential violation. The ratio of the quantum value to the noncontextual bound of the full inequality satisfies \(L/(L-2d) \ge (D+2)/(2\sqrt{D+1}) \sim 2^{N/2-1}\). Cabello [C] proposed this inequality in 2010 and argued for exponential growth; his Lemma 3 identifies the bound with the number of positive contexts, which is correct for two qubits but not for three, where 252 of the 315 predictions can be satisfied rather than 225 [M1]. Corollary A proves the growth.

Noise. If imperfections scale every correlator by a visibility \(\eta\), the inequality is violated when \(\eta \gt 1 - 2d/L\), hence whenever \(\eta \gt 2\sqrt{D+1}/(D+2)\): above \(0.6\) for three qubits, \(0.458\) for four, \(0.338\) for five, \(0.062\) for ten. In the notation of [C, MS] the tolerated error per context \(\varepsilon = 2d/L\) is at least \(1 - 2\sqrt{D+1}/(D+2)\), which exceeds \(0.707\) (the largest value confirmed in the search of [MS], for configurations of a different kind) from six qubits on and tends to \(1\).

Proposition 4 (the floor is sharp up to a logarithmic factor). For every pure \(N\)-qubit state with \(M = \max_{v \ne 0} |\langle P_v \rangle|\) there is an assignment of the full space with

\[\beta \;\ge\; \frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{6\,M^3}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \frac{1}{(D+1)(D+2)\,M_N^3} \;\le\; \frac12 - \frac{d}{L} \;\le\; \frac{\sqrt{D+1}}{D+2},\]

where \(M_N\) is the least value of \(M\) over all states. \(M_N \ge 1/\sqrt{D+1}\), with equality exactly when a Pauli-covariant SIC exists, and \(M_N \le C\sqrt{\log D / D}\) for an absolute constant \(C\).

Proof. A pure state has \(\operatorname{tr}\rho^3 = 1\); with \(\rho = D^{-1}(I + \sum_v p_v P_v)\) and \(\sum_{v\ne 0} p_v^2 = D-1\), the trace computation of Theorem 1 gives \(T(p) = \sum_\ell s_\ell\, p_u p_v p_w = (D-1)(D-2)/6\) (the cubic purity condition [JL, St]). Choose the signs \(f(v)\) independently with mean \(p_v/M\). Then \(\mathbb{E}\,\beta(f) = T(p)/M^3\), and some \(f\) does at least as well. The upper bound on \(M_N\) follows from Lévy's lemma for Haar-random states and a union bound over the \(D^2-1\) observables [HLW]. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

So \(\tfrac12 - d/L = \Theta(2^{-N/2})\) up to a factor \(\log^{3/2} D\). In four sample runs the sign patterns of Haar-random states reached between 50 and 64 per cent of the floor value of \(\beta\) for four and five qubits. We did not find Proposition 4 in the notes we could read; we do not claim it is new.

Averaging between families. The main theorem, as checked:

theorem hoggar_bound (hN : 1 ≤ N) (S : Finset (Pt N)) (h0 : (0 : Pt N) ∉ S) (f : Pt N → ℝ)
    (hf : ∀ u ∈ S, f u = 1 ∨ f u = -1) :
    ((2 : ℝ) ^ N - 1) * beta6 S f
      ≤ ((2 : ℝ) ^ N - 2) * S.card * Real.sqrt (((2 : ℝ) ^ N - 1) * S.card)

Here beta6 S f is six times \(\beta_S(f)\), a sum over ordered pairs, and the sign of a context is by definition the real part of the scalar \(\tau(u,v)\) with pauli u * pauli v * pauli (u + v) = tau u v • 1. Table 2 lists the corollaries.

How the exact degrees are checked. The statement of pivot_degree is about a set of 135 explicit complex \(16 \times 16\) matrices, given by a 256-bit mask: bit \(a + 16c\) says whether \(P_{(a|c)}\) belongs to it. MaskConfig.lean proves, for every \(N\) and every mask, that the number of flags of the configuration and the number of flags violated by an assignment that is itself given by a mask are double sums over codes of Boolean expressions built from bit tests, exclusive or and addition of natural numbers. The kernel evaluates these sums (decide +kernel); for five qubits each sum has \(2^{20}\) terms and is evaluated in 16 blocks. degree_of_certificate then combines three evaluated numbers (observables, flags, flags violated by the witness) with hoggar_bound. That the masks are the supports and sign patterns of the states of Table 3 is checked by the programs of Section 10 in exact arithmetic; it is not needed for, and not part of, the Lean statements. For seven qubits the two sums have \(2^{28}\) terms each and were not evaluated in the kernel: the degree \(729\,344\) of \(S_7\) rests on hoggar_bound, which needs only the number 8128, and on a count of contexts and violated contexts made by programs.

What is not formalized. Theorem 2 (the quaternionic form) and all elliptic-quadric numbers; Theorems 3 and 4 as equivalences, and every statement about states, spectra or geometry (the Lean results are the inequality and the two-sided degree statements); Propositions 4, 6 and 8 to 11; the enumerations of Theorems 12 and 13 and the seven-qubit counts; the limit statement as a theorem about limits (the inequality for each \(N\) is formal); the exact values of [J1]. For the classical families beyond three qubits and \(Q^+(7,2)\), upper bounds are witnesses checked by the programs of Section 10.

A separate set of agent sessions, working concurrently in the same repository and without editing our files, re-checked the first six modules from source, confirmed the axiom report, and added two modules of its own (IndependentContexts.lean, IndependentWitness.lean). They prove that a violated context carries exactly six violated flags (used in MaskConfig.lean) and, by kernel evaluation of an integer certificate, that the explicit assignment "\(f(a,c) = +1\) if \(a = 0\) or \(c = 0\) or the number of \(Y\) factors is at least 2, and \(-1\) otherwise" violates exactly 63 contexts. 9. A running example on the Tsotchke stack

All four components are used for what they are.

  • Moonlab (v1.2.1, prebuilt WebAssembly engine) is a classical state-vector simulator. It plays the quantum device.
  • quantum_rng is a classical, "quantum-inspired" integer and trigonometric mixer, not a hardware quantum source. It supplies seeded pseudo-random numbers. Its stream depends on the C library it is built against, so no result depends on its exact values: every count is recomputed independently.

10. Falsifiability and replication

Predictions that could fail. (P1) No assignment violates fewer contexts than the floor. (P2) The saturated cases are reached exactly. (P3) For every \(S\) and \(f\), \(\operatorname{tr}A = 0\), \(\operatorname{tr}A^2 = D|S|\), \(\operatorname{tr}A^3 = 6D\beta\). (P4) On an elliptic quadric the spectrum of \(A\) is doubly degenerate. (P5) For every pure state \(T(p) = (D-1)(D-2)/6\). (P6) Ideal sequential measurements reproduce every context sign in every state. (P7) For a flat state, condition (d) of Theorem 4 holds. A single counterexample to P1 would be an assignment whose violation count anyone can recompute in milliseconds.

Tests (scripts/run_all.sh).

  • hoggar exhaust2: all 32768 two-qubit assignments; minimum 3 (640 assignments); for the square, minimum 1.
  • scripts/run_falsify.sh: 20 runs over \(N = 2, \dots, 5\) and the three families; 20000, 5000 and 500 uniformly random assignments and 1500, 200 and 12 annealed ones per configuration for \(N = 3, 4, 5\). No assignment below the floor. Minima reached by annealing: 63, 21, 9 (\(N=3\)); 1575, 315, 315 (\(N=4\)); 31479, 6975, 7087 (\(N=5\)). These equal the best known values in every case.
  • A negative control: the false claim \(d(W(5,2)) \ge 64\) is refuted by the Hoggar witness with exit code 3, showing that the harness can fail.
  • scripts/flat_states.py (68 checks, integer arithmetic in \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) except one labelled check): for the six states of Table 3, the single modulus, the support, the number of contexts, the number violated, the floor, condition (d) at every point and the vanishing of every outside sum; the statements of Theorem 5 about \(S_B\); Proposition 6 on all 4096 phase patterns; Proposition 9 for one to four qubits; and an annealing search from random starts on \(S_B\) and \(S_5\), which reaches 75 and 480 and never goes below.
  • build/third_level (seven seconds): the enumeration of Theorem 12, all 33 554 431 forms for five qubits included, with the orbit computation; it fails if any count differs from the table.
  • scripts/phase_states.py (81 checks, integer arithmetic): for \(CS|{+}{+}\rangle\), \(CCZ|{+}{+}{+}\rangle\), \(CS\,CS|{+}{+}{+}\rangle\), \(\Phi_5\) and \(\Phi_7\), the constant rank, the single modulus, the support, the numbers of contexts and violated contexts, condition (d) and the outside sums, and the description of the support in Proposition 11; Proposition 10 for all eleven states of this paper; the enumeration of Theorem 12 for \(N \le 4\) by a second implementation; the two symplectic maps of Theorem 12; and the finite-field descriptions of the five- and seven-qubit forms.

11. Limitations and open problems

  • Novelty is claimed to the extent of a bounded search (arXiv and Zenodo queries on 9 November 2026, and the sources of Section 1.2). The field moved quickly in October 2026, and several of the relevant preprints are less than a month old. Statements of the form "to our knowledge" mean exactly that.
  • The configurations \(S_B\), \(S_5\) and \(S_7\) are defined by states, not by a classical geometric construction. We do not know an infinite family of non-closed configurations that attain the floor: Proposition 9 shows that the simplest family of states stops at four qubits, and for flat phase states we have three, five and seven qubits only, with nine open. Theorem 12 is complete for its class of states; whether Table 3 is complete for all states on \(N \le 5\) qubits is supported by numerical search only. Whether the states of the odd class of Proposition 6 form a single Clifford orbit was not determined.
  • For seven qubits the uniqueness statement is proved only inside the family \(\mathcal{F}\) of Theorem 13; outside it there is a random census. The enumeration behind it (and the one of Theorem 12) is a computation, repeated here by one program only, apart from the agreement of the count with the orbit size and the second implementation for \(N \le 4\). The seven-qubit counts of contexts are checked by three programs but not by the Lean kernel.
  • Within the classical families the floor is not attained beyond the three listed cases [J2]. Exact values for five or more qubits are open except \(Q^+(9,2)\) [J1]; the floor alone leaves the intervals of Table 1.
  • The exact four- and five-qubit values of the classical families rest on computer-assisted work that is neither formally verified nor peer reviewed [J1]. A formal proof of \(d(W(7,2)) = 1575\) would be a natural next target; our Lean development gives the lower bound 1451 only.
  • The quaternionic form (Theorem 2) and the equivalences of Theorems 3 and 4 are proved on paper and tested numerically, not formalized.
  • Nothing here is an experiment on quantum hardware, and nothing shows a computational advantage. The simulation demonstrates what quantum mechanics predicts and what no noncontextual model can do.
